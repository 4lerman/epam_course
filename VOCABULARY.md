# GenAI Vocabulary Dictionary

This dictionary contains essential terms and concepts for understanding Generative AI applications as a subject matter expert (SME).

---

## 1. **Generative AI**
Artificial intelligence systems that can create new content (text, images, code, etc.) rather than just analyzing or classifying existing data. These models learn patterns from training data and generate novel outputs based on user prompts.

## 2. **Hallucination**
When an AI model confidently generates false, misleading, or nonsensical information that appears plausible but has no basis in its training data or reality. A critical challenge in text generation requiring fact-checking.

## 3. **Prompt**
The input text or instruction given to a generative AI model to guide what content it should create. Prompt quality directly impacts output quality—clear, specific prompts yield better results.

## 4. **Text-to-Image**
A category of generative AI models that create visual content from natural language descriptions. Users describe what they want to see, and the model generates corresponding images.

## 5. **Boilerplate Code**
Repetitive, standard code sections that must be written in many places with little variation. AI code assistants excel at generating this type of code, saving developers significant time.

## 6. **A/B Testing**
A method of comparing two versions of content (A and B) to determine which performs better. Generative AI enables rapid creation of multiple variants for testing marketing copy and designs.

## 7. **SEO (Search Engine Optimization)**
The practice of improving content to rank higher in search engine results. Generative AI can scale content creation for SEO but must balance quantity with quality and accuracy.

## 8. **Deepfake**
Synthetic media where AI is used to replace or manipulate a person's likeness in images or videos, often used maliciously. A significant ethical concern with generative image and video technology.

## 9. **Training Data**
The large collection of examples (text, images, code, etc.) used to teach a generative AI model patterns and relationships. The quality and bias in training data directly affect model outputs.

## 10. **Bias**
Systematic prejudices or stereotypes present in AI outputs, typically inherited from biased patterns in training data. A major concern in image generation where models may reinforce harmful stereotypes.

## 11. **Fine-tuning**
The process of further training a pre-trained AI model on specific, specialized data to adapt it for particular tasks or domains, improving performance for specific use cases.

## 12. **Token**
The basic unit of text processing in AI models—typically a word, part of a word, or punctuation mark. Models process and generate text token by token, and API costs are often charged per token.

## 13. **Model Inference**
The process of using a trained AI model to generate predictions or outputs based on new input. This is the "production" phase after training is complete.
